# The huge door: an art brief

For ChatGPT to draw **four views of the huge door: front, back, left and
right**. Tripo3D then turns those views into one 3D model. Section 5 holds the
prompts to paste. The door is **all metal and plumbing**: a sewer's sluice
door, made of riveted steel, pipes, pistons and gauges. There is no wood and no
stone on it.

---

## 1. What it is in the game

*Last Light* is a small horror game. A kid of about 8 (1.2 m tall, with an
orange pumpkin head and a burning stick for a torch) wakes up in a sewer full of
rats and has to escape. In the sewer's round central chamber stands the **huge
door**, the only way on. Three heavy steel bars lock it:

- the **left bar** is released by a valve lever at the end of the left tunnel
  (the gas tunnel);
- the **right bar** is released by a valve lever at the end of the right
  tunnel (the chandelier tunnel);
- the **central bar** is lifted by the **hub lever**, which stands a few metres
  in front of the door. The two side bars hook over the central bar and pin
  it, **so the hub lever cannot move until both side bars are up.**

The door has to tell the player this with no text: **two bars from either
side lie across one bar in the middle. Each side bar is worked by a pipe that
runs off toward its own tunnel.**

## 2. The door, part by part (in metres; the kid is 1.2 m)

**The frame.** A heavy steel portal, like an industrial sluice gate. Two
riveted steel I-beam pillars and a riveted lintel beam make a doorway 4.8 m
wide and 3.5 m high. The whole frame is about 5.6 m wide, 4.0 m tall and 0.5 m
deep. Bolted flanges join the beams, and rows of big rivets run along every
edge. A thick steel sill plate lies on the ground.

**The two leaves.** Two steel door leaves, each 2.4 m wide and 3.4 m tall,
meet at a vertical seam in the middle. Each is made of riveted steel plates on
a grid of heavy stiffening ribs: three horizontal ribs and one vertical rib per
leaf. Big hinge knuckles on their outer edges show on the back. Grime and
water lines darken the bottom 0.5 m.

**The central bar.** A thick vertical square steel bar, 0.25 m wide, stands on
the seam in front of the leaves. Two big guide brackets, one riveted to each
leaf, hold it. Its foot sits in a steel socket in the sill. Its top goes up
into a **hydraulic cylinder** bolted under the lintel's middle, which lifts it
1 m when the hub lever is worked. Two short round **pins** stick out of its
front face, a low one at 1.2 m and a high one at 2.2 m.

**The left bar.** A horizontal steel beam, 0.2 m square and 2.6 m long,
hinged on the left pillar at 1.2 m height. Its free end is a big **downturned
hook** that drops over the central bar's low pin. Under its hinge, a vertical
**piston** (a steel cylinder about 1 m tall, mounted on the pillar's front)
pushes it up. The piston is fed by a **pipe** that leaves the cylinder's base,
bends outward and runs **away to the left**, about 1 m past the frame, ending
in a bolted flange. A round **pressure gauge** with a glass face sits on that
pipe. On the beam's middle, a round emblem plate is stencilled in
**bone-white paint**: a pipe nozzle puffing a gas cloud.

**The right bar.** The mirror of the left one, on the right pillar, at
**2.2 m** height. Its hook drops over the central bar's high pin, and its
piston's pipe runs **away to the right**, ending in a flange 1 m past the
frame, with its own gauge. Its emblem is **a hanging chandelier**.

The two side bars sit at different heights so they don't collide and both
stay visible.

**The central pipe.** A pipe comes out of the ground in front of the door's
middle, through a flange in the sill, and climbs the left side of the seam to
the central bar's cylinder under the lintel. The hub lever drives this pipe,
from a few metres in front of the door, so it must read as the central bar's
own pipe. Valves on it are decoration: use **no handwheels anywhere**, because
the player would try to turn them.

**Plumbing dressing.** Some extra pipes, elbows, flanges and bolted
inspection hatches round the frame make it read as part of the sewer's
machinery. Keep them **behind or beside the bars**, never in front of them: the
three bars and their hooks must stay the clearest shapes on the door.

**The state drawn: LOCKED.** Both side bars lie **flat and horizontal**
across the leaves, their hooks over the central bar's pins. Their pistons are
retracted and the central bar is down in its socket. (Released, a side bar
swings **up** through 90° and stands upright against its pillar, like a
barrier arm.)

## 3. What each view shows

| View | What the camera sees |
|---|---|
| **Front** (the side the kid faces) | The whole mechanism, flat on: frame, both leaves, the central bar, both side bars lying across with their hooks on the pins, both pistons, the emblems, the side pipes leaving left and right, the central pipe and its cylinder |
| **Back** | The other face of the leaves: riveted plates, the rib grid, the hinge knuckles on the outer edges, the back of the frame. None of the mechanism shows except the pipe flanges sticking out on either side |
| **Left** | The door in profile from its left: the frame's depth (0.5 m), the leaves' thickness (about 0.3 m), and everything on the front standing out toward the front. The left piston and the hinge of the left bar are nearest the camera; the left pipe points straight at the camera, so its end flange is seen face on |
| **Right** | The mirror of the left view: the right piston, the right bar's hinge, and the right pipe's flange seen face on |

## 4. Style and colour

- **Style:** a stylized, hand-painted game prop, chunky and slightly
  exaggerated, like Dofus or *Penguins of Madagascar* concept art: thick
  bars, big rivets, oversized hooks, bold shapes.
- **Steel:** dark blackened steel, wet and grimy.
- **Pipes:** old copper with heavy **teal-green verdigris**, and dark steel
  flanges.
- **Gauges:** dull, dark brass with pale faces.
- **Weathering:** green and teal verdigris, limescale, dark water stains,
  a little moss in the corners. **No orange rust and no orange anywhere**
  (the kid's pumpkin head is the scene's only orange).
- **Emblems:** stencilled in bone-white paint, the brightest values on the
  door.
- **No text, no numbers, no graffiti, no gore.**

## 5. The prompts

### Prompt 1: the four views on one sheet (start with this)

> A character-turnaround-style reference sheet for a single 3D game prop: a
> huge industrial sewer sluice door, all metal and plumbing. Four
> **orthographic** views in a 2 × 2 grid, the same scale, aligned at the
> ground line: **FRONT** (top left), **BACK** (top right), **LEFT side**
> (bottom left), **RIGHT side** (bottom right). Plain light-grey background,
> flat even lighting, no cast shadows, no perspective, no environment, no
> characters, no labels or text. Stylized hand-painted game-asset look,
> chunky exaggerated proportions, clean readable shapes.
>
> The door: a heavy riveted steel portal frame (I-beam pillars and lintel,
> bolted flanges, a steel sill plate) around a doorway 4.8 m wide and 3.5 m
> high. Two riveted steel door leaves with a grid of stiffening ribs meet at a
> vertical seam. On the seam, in front, a thick vertical square steel bar sits
> in a floor socket, its top in a hydraulic cylinder under the lintel. Two
> short round pins stick out of its front, one low and one high. On the left
> pillar, a horizontal steel beam is hinged at waist height and lies flat
> across the left leaf, its big downturned hook over the central bar's low
> pin. A vertical piston on the pillar sits under its hinge, and a verdigris
> copper pipe with a round pressure gauge leaves the piston and runs off to
> the left, ending in a bolted flange. The right pillar mirrors this, with its
> beam hinged higher up, hooked over the high pin, and its pipe running off to
> the right. On the left beam, a round plate stencilled bone-white with a gas
> cloud puffing from a nozzle; on the right beam, a round plate stencilled
> bone-white with a hanging chandelier. A third copper pipe rises from the
> ground at the door's middle and climbs to the central bar's cylinder. Extra
> pipes, elbows, flanges and bolted hatches round the frame, kept behind or
> beside the bars. No handwheels.
>
> The back view shows only riveted plates, ribs, hinge knuckles and the pipe
> flanges sticking out on either side. The side views show the door in
> profile: frame depth, leaf thickness, the pistons and hinges standing out
> toward the front, and the side pipe's end flange seen face on.
>
> Colours: dark blackened wet steel, copper pipes with heavy teal-green
> verdigris, dull dark brass gauges, limescale and dark water stains, a little
> moss. No orange, no rust, no text, no gore.

### Prompts 2 to 5: one view per picture (if Tripo3D needs separate images)

Paste prompt 1 first, then in **the same chat**, ask for each view as its own
picture so the design stays the same:

> **2.** Now draw only the FRONT view of this exact door, on its own: one
> orthographic image, the door centred and filling the frame, plain light-grey
> background, flat lighting, no shadows, nothing cropped. Keep every detail
> identical to the sheet.

> **3.** The same for the BACK view of this exact door.

> **4.** The same for the LEFT side view of this exact door.

> **5.** The same for the RIGHT side view of this exact door.

If a view drifts from the others (a missing pipe, a bar at another height),
say exactly what differs and ask for it again. Tripo3D gets confused by views
that don't match.

### Prompt 6: the hub lever (separate prop, the same four views)

Replaced by `lever-brief.md`, which draws the three levers as one family.

> In the same style, a turnaround sheet of a second prop: the sewer lever
> that drives the door's central bar. Four orthographic views in a 2 × 2 grid
> (front, back, left, right), the same scale, plain light-grey background,
> flat lighting, no shadows, no text. A squat riveted steel pump housing,
> about 0.6 × 0.6 × 0.5 m, with a big brass pressure gauge on its front. A
> thick steel lever arm rises 1.6 m from its top and ends in a horizontal
> grip wrapped in dark rubber or cloth, wide enough for two small hands.
> A toothed steel ratchet arc with a pawl sits on its side. A verdigris
> copper pipe leaves the housing's base at the front, low, and bends down to
> go into the floor. Same colours: blackened steel, teal verdigris copper,
> dull brass, no orange, no rust, no handwheels.

## 6. After Tripo3D (Blender)

- **Moving parts:** Tripo3D gives back **one fused mesh**. The game needs the
  leaves, the central bar, the two side bars and the lever arm as separate
  objects. Cut them apart in Blender, or generate each one alone with the
  same prompts ("only the left beam with its hook and emblem, four views...").
- **Pivots:**
  - each side bar: at its hinge, rotating 90° upward;
  - the central bar: sliding 1 m straight up;
  - each leaf: at its outer edge, swinging away from the front;
  - the lever arm: at the housing, tilting about 60° forward.
- **Fit to the guide (`exports/sewers.glb`):**
  - The doorway runs from x -2.4 to 2.4 on the line z -19.4, with its front
    facing the hub (south, +z).
  - The hub lever stands at (0, -15.6), facing the door.
  - The left (gas) bar is on the west side; the right (water) bar is on the
    east side.
  - Name the parts after the guide's objects: `huge door`,
    `huge door.rod.gas`, `huge door.rod.water`, `huge door.rod.hub`,
    `lever.hub`.
- **The pipe flanges** at the ends of the side pipes are where the pipes
  along the chamber's walls join on, toward each tunnel. They're modelled with
  the room, not with the door.

# The fallen trunk: an art brief

A brief for ChatGPT, which draws **four separate pictures of one fallen
trunk** (front, left side, right side and back), and then for Tripo3D, which
turns those four pictures into a 3D model. Section 4 holds the prompts to
paste, in order, in **one chat**. **One model** serves before and after the
strike: the fire is a game effect, so the pictures show no flame, and no
charring.

**The reference:** a screenshot of the dead tree
(`public/models/deadTree.glb`), so the trunk is drawn in the same hand and
reads as the same kind of dead wood. Attach it to prompt 1.

---

## 1. What it is in the game

*Last Light*'s goose game: a goose, a burning torch in her beak, crosses a
night plain on All Hallows' Eve, from light to light, gathering her goslings.
Rats fill the dark; only fire holds them off, and her torch burns down. She
fills it again at each flame, touching her torch to it.

The plain's props come from a **Halloween village**, crooked and a little
sinister, in the spirit of Tim Burton and *MediEvil*: the old well, its
lantern on a gibbet, carved pumpkins, the dead tree.

**The fallen trunk** is a light of its own. A dead tree blew down long ago
and lies on the plain, its roots torn up out of the ground and standing on
end. It is dark until a scripted **lightning strike** hits its upturned
roots and sets it burning: from then on it is a fire like the braziers,
holding the rats off, and she fills her torch at it. A **gosling hides
inside** its hollow end.

What the trunk needs to read as:

- **a tree that fell:** the upturned root plate at one end tells it at once,
  and gives the strike a high point to hit that a log on the ground lacks;
- **hollow:** a dark, open end a gosling has crept into, plain from far off;
- **easy to read from far off:** the root plate's ragged disc against the
  night sky, before the strike lights it.

## 2. The trunk (in metres; the goose is 0.9 m tall)

A **simple** prop with only four parts. Nothing else.

1. **The root plate:** at one end, the roots torn up with the earth still
   in them, standing on end like a ragged wheel, about **1.8 m tall and
   1.8 m wide** and about **0.4 m thick**, its lower edge sunk into the
   ground. Thick roots reach out of it like crooked fingers, a few broken
   off short; clods of dark earth and a few stones are caught between them.
2. **The trunk:** lying on the ground from the root plate, about
   **2.8 m long**, about **0.85 m thick** at the roots and **0.65 m** at
   its other end, a little crooked along its length, its belly sunk a
   little into the ground. Its top stands at about **0.8 m**, just under
   the goose's head.
3. **The hollow end:** the far end, where the tree broke off, ragged and
   splintered round its rim, its heart rotted out into a dark **round hole
   about 0.4 m across**, reaching about **1 m** into the trunk. A gosling
   fits inside; the goose does not.
4. **The branch:** one thick broken-off branch, rising from the trunk's top
   about **halfway along**, leaning toward the **front** and ending in a
   snapped point at about **1.3 m**. It is the one detail that tells the
   front from the back.

The whole model is about **3.2 m long, 1.8 m wide** (the root plate) **and
1.8 m tall**.

**Its age:** the wood under the bark is weathered silver-grey; the bark is
dark, cracked into plates, peeling off in patches; pale moss and lichen on
the trunk's top and in the root plate's hollows; the hollow's inside is dark,
crumbly, rotten heartwood.

## 3. Style and colour

- **Style:** a stylized, hand-painted game prop, chunky and slightly
  exaggerated, crooked, like the dead tree, *MediEvil*'s remake or a Tim
  Burton village. Big simple shapes, few details, a clean, spiky silhouette
  against the night.
- **Colours:**
  - **wood:** weathered silver-grey where the bark is gone, dark grey-brown
    bark;
  - **roots and earth:** dark brown-black, a few cold grey stones;
  - **moss and lichen:** pale, dusty grey-green;
  - **the hollow:** near black inside.
- **Leave out:** fire, flame, glow, smoke, charring, scorch marks, faces in
  the bark, pumpkins, leaves, mushrooms, the gosling, text, and any
  ornaments.

## 4. The prompts

Paste them one after the other in **the same chat**, so ChatGPT keeps the
same trunk from picture to picture. **Attach the dead tree's screenshot to
prompt 1.**

### Prompt 1: the front view, and how the four pictures work

> I need reference pictures of one 3D game prop so that a 3D tool (Tripo3D)
> can rebuild it. I will ask you for **four separate pictures, one at a time,
> each in its own image**: the FRONT, the LEFT side, the RIGHT side and the
> BACK. Please do NOT put several views in one image, and do not make a sheet
> or a grid.
>
> The rules for all four pictures:
> - exactly the same object, same size and same position in the frame, centred
>   and fully visible from end to end and from the ground to the top, nothing
>   cropped;
> - a perfectly straight-on orthographic view, with no perspective, no
>   three-quarter angle, and the camera at the object's mid-height;
> - a plain light-grey background, flat even lighting, no cast shadows, no
>   environment, no other objects, no text or labels.
>
> The attached picture is a dead tree from the same game, a Halloween village
> plain at night: draw the trunk **in the same style**, so the two belong
> together. Do not draw that tree.
>
> The prop: a dead tree that fell over long ago, lying on the ground, in a
> stylized hand-painted game-asset style, chunky and slightly exaggerated, in
> the spirit of Tim Burton and MediEvil. It is about 3.2 m long and 1.8 m
> tall and has only four parts:
> 1. at ONE end, the root plate: the roots torn up out of the ground with the
>    earth still in them, standing on end like a ragged wheel about 1.8 m
>    tall and wide, its lower edge sunk in the ground, thick crooked roots
>    reaching out of it like fingers, clods of dark earth and a few stones
>    caught between them;
> 2. the trunk, lying on the ground from the root plate, about 2.8 m long,
>    thicker at the roots and thinner at the other end, a little crooked,
>    its top about as high as half the root plate;
> 3. at the OTHER end, where the tree broke off, a ragged splintered rim
>    round a dark round HOLE: the trunk is hollow there, its rotten heart
>    gone, the hole big enough for a small bird to creep into;
> 4. one thick broken-off branch rising from the trunk's top about halfway
>    along, leaning toward the FRONT, ending in a snapped point.
>
> The wood is weathered silver-grey where the bark is gone; the bark is dark
> grey-brown, cracked into plates and peeling in patches; pale grey-green
> moss and lichen on the trunk's top; near black inside the hollow.
>
> It has NOT burned: no fire, no glow, no smoke, no charring, no scorch
> marks. No faces in the bark, no pumpkins, no leaves, no mushrooms, no
> animals, no text.
>
> Now draw **picture 1 of 4: the FRONT view**, looking at the trunk's long
> side, the root plate at the LEFT of the picture and the hollow end at the
> RIGHT, the branch leaning toward the camera.

### Prompt 2: the left side

> Now **picture 2 of 4: the LEFT side view** of exactly the same trunk. The
> camera has moved round to the HOLLOW end and looks straight down the
> trunk's length: the dark round hole and its splintered rim face the
> camera in the middle of the picture, and the root plate stands behind the
> trunk, its ragged disc and roots showing all round it. The branch rises
> above the trunk, leaning to the RIGHT of the picture. Keep the same scale,
> background and lighting as picture 1, straight-on and orthographic.

### Prompt 3: the right side

> Now **picture 3 of 4: the RIGHT side view** of exactly the same trunk. The
> camera has moved round to the ROOT end and looks straight at the root
> plate's underside, the side that was in the ground: a wide ragged disc of
> tangled roots, dark earth and stones fills the middle of the picture, and
> the trunk is hidden behind it. Keep everything else identical to
> picture 1.

### Prompt 4: the back

> Now **picture 4 of 4: the BACK view** of exactly the same trunk, the camera
> turned 180° from the front: the trunk's other long side, the root plate
> now at the RIGHT of the picture and the hollow end at the LEFT, the branch
> leaning AWAY from the camera, mostly behind the trunk, its snapped point
> showing above it. Keep everything else identical to picture 1.

**If a picture drifts** (fire, glow or char appearing, the trunk standing
instead of lying, the roots in the ground instead of torn up, the hole
closed or at the root end, the branch missing or leaning back in the front
view, a face in the bark, leaves or mushrooms added, an angle instead of
straight on, two views in one image), say exactly what is wrong and ask for
that picture again. Tripo3D needs the four views to match.

## 5. After Tripo3D (Blender)

- **The pivot** goes on the ground under the trunk's middle.
- **The fire** comes from the game, not from the model. Mark where it
  burns with an empty named `fire`, on the trunk's top by the root plate,
  at about **0.8 m**, where the goose can reach it with her torch.
- **The strike:** mark the root plate's top with an empty named `strike`,
  where the bolt comes down.
- **The gosling:** mark the hollow's mouth with an empty named `hollow`,
  facing out along the trunk, so the game knows where its gosling hides and
  comes out.
- **The hollow** stays open, about 0.4 m across and 1 m deep, with no faces
  closing it, so a gosling set inside shows from the mouth.
- **A collider**, as the well's and the cage's: a few boxes round the trunk
  and the root plate, named `ColliderTrunk`, leaving the hollow's mouth
  clear.
- **The scale:** the goose is 0.9 m tall; the trunk's top comes up to about
  her neck, the root plate twice her height.

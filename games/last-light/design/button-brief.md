# The symbol door's buttons: an art brief

A brief for ChatGPT, which draws **four separate pictures of one button
post** (front, left side, right side and back), and then for Tripo3D, which
turns those four pictures into a 3D model. Section 4 holds the prompts to
paste, in order, in **one chat**. **One model** is made, and the game places
it four times.

The buttons are drawn **blank**. Their symbols (key, fish, skull, wheel) are
added later, so the button's face is left empty for them.

---

## 1. What it is in the game

*Last Light* is a small horror game. A kid of about 8 (1.2 m tall, with an
orange pumpkin head and a burning stick for a torch) has to escape a sewer
full of rats.

Near the end he reaches the **cistern**: an old round water tank, 14 m across,
long since drained and full of rats. Four plates in its floor each show a
numeral and a symbol. In the cistern's north face stands the **symbol door**,
the way out, and in front of it a row of **four small posts**, each with a
button bearing one symbol. He presses them in the plates' order, and the door
opens.

What the post needs to read as:

- **a little brother of the cistern's pillars:** the same hexagonal stone, the
  same iron, at a third of the size, so it belongs to the place;
- **part of the door's machinery:** an iron rod runs from it into the floor,
  toward the door, as if the button worked the lock;
- **something to press:** one big round button on a sloping head, turned to
  the kid as he walks up.

## 2. The post (in metres; the kid is 1.2 m)

A **simple** prop with only four parts. Nothing else.

1. **The shaft:** a plain **hexagonal** stone post, about **0.28 m across**
   (flat face to flat face) and about **0.9 m tall**, of two or three big
   soft-edged blocks, with six flat faces. One flat face is the **front**.
   A low hexagonal plinth at its foot, 0.08 m tall, no wider than 0.35 m.
2. **The head:** a hexagonal block of blackened iron on top, about 0.15 m
   tall and a little wider than the shaft (up to 0.36 m). Its top is cut on a
   **slope, about 30°, falling toward the front**, so the button looks up
   and out at the kid.
3. **The button:** one big round push button, about **0.18 m across**, set in
   the middle of the sloping top, standing a little proud of it. Dull dark
   bronze, a little brighter than the rest, so it catches the torchlight. Its
   face is **flat, smooth and empty**: no symbol, no letter, no number, no
   carving. The symbol goes there later.
4. **The rod:** one iron rod, about 0.04 m across, down the **back face
   only**, from under the head into the floor, held by one small bracket.
   With the slope, it tells the front from the back.

The whole model is about **0.36 m wide and 1.05 m tall**: the button sits at
the kid's chest.

**The water's marks:** the whole post stood under the water line, so its
stone is the darker, green-brown stained stone of the pillars' lower half,
with a pale limescale crust and a little moss at the foot. The iron head is
blackened with teal-green verdigris at its edges. The **button is cleaner**,
worn smooth, as if hands still pressed it.

## 3. Style and colour

- **Style:** a stylized, hand-painted game prop, chunky and slightly
  exaggerated, like Dofus or *Penguins of Madagascar* concept art. Big simple
  shapes, soft edges, few details, clean silhouette. The same family as the
  pillars.
- **Colours:**
  - **stone:** sage and blue-green, darkened and stained green-brown;
  - **head and rod:** blackened iron with teal-green verdigris;
  - **button:** dull dark bronze, worn smooth;
  - a pale crust of limescale at the foot.
- **Leave out:** orange, rust, gold, text, numbers, symbols, carvings,
  engravings, runes, and any ornaments.

## 4. The prompts

Paste them one after the other in **the same chat**, so ChatGPT keeps the
same post from picture to picture.

### Prompt 1: the front view, and how the four pictures work

> I need reference pictures of one 3D game prop so that a 3D tool (Tripo3D)
> can rebuild it. I will ask you for **four separate pictures, one at a time,
> each in its own image**: the FRONT, the LEFT side, the RIGHT side and the
> BACK. Please do NOT put several views in one image, and do not make a sheet
> or a grid.
>
> The rules for all four pictures:
> - exactly the same object, same size and same position in the frame, centred
>   and fully visible from the floor to the top, nothing cropped;
> - a perfectly straight-on orthographic view, with no perspective, no
>   three-quarter angle, and the camera at the object's mid-height;
> - a plain light-grey background, flat even lighting, no cast shadows, no
>   environment, no other objects, no text or labels.
>
> The prop: a small button post from an old drained underground water
> cistern, in a stylized hand-painted game-asset style (like Dofus), chunky,
> with big simple shapes and few details. It is about 1 m tall and has only
> four parts:
> 1. a plain HEXAGONAL stone post of two or three big soft-edged blocks, six
>    flat faces, about 0.28 m across and 0.9 m tall, on a low hexagonal plinth
>    barely wider than the post;
> 2. a hexagonal head of blackened iron on top, a little wider than the post,
>    its top cut on a slope of about 30° falling toward the FRONT;
> 3. one big round push button of dull dark bronze, about two thirds of the
>    head's width, set in the middle of the sloping top. Its face is flat,
>    smooth and EMPTY: no symbol, no letter, no number, no carving;
> 4. one thin iron rod down the BACK face only, from under the head into the
>    floor, held by one small bracket.
>
> The stone is dark sage and blue-green, stained green-brown from years under
> water, with a pale limescale crust and a little moss at the foot. The iron
> head is blackened, with teal-green verdigris at its edges. The button is
> cleaner, worn smooth.
>
> No orange, no rust, no gold, no carvings, no ornaments.
>
> Now draw **picture 1 of 4: the FRONT view**, looking straight at the flat
> face under the button, so the slope of the head falls toward the camera and
> the button's round face is seen from the front, foreshortened into a wide
> oval.

### Prompt 2: the left side

> Now **picture 2 of 4: the LEFT side view** of exactly the same post. The
> camera has turned 90° to the post's left. The post is hexagonal, so a
> CORNER now points at the camera, with two faces sloping away on either
> side. The head's slope is seen in profile, falling toward the LEFT, the
> button standing proud of it, and the rod runs down the RIGHT edge. Keep the
> same size, position, background and lighting as picture 1, straight-on and
> orthographic.

### Prompt 3: the right side

> Now **picture 3 of 4: the RIGHT side view** of exactly the same post. The
> camera has turned 90° to the post's right. A corner points at the camera,
> the head's slope falls toward the RIGHT, and the rod runs down the LEFT
> edge. Keep everything else identical to picture 1.

### Prompt 4: the back

> Now **picture 4 of 4: the BACK view** of exactly the same post, the camera
> turned 180° from the front, looking straight at the flat face with the rod,
> so the rod runs down the middle of the picture. The head's top slopes away
> from the camera, so the button is hidden or barely shows over the high
> back edge. Keep everything else identical to picture 1.

**If a picture drifts** (a symbol or mark on the button, the post round or
square instead of hexagonal, the head flat instead of sloping, the slope or
the rod on another face, an angle instead of straight on, two views in one
image), say exactly what is wrong and ask for that picture again. Tripo3D
needs the four views to match.

## 5. After Tripo3D (Blender)

- **The pivot** goes at the middle of the post's foot, on the floor.
- **The button** is cut apart from the head in Blender, its pivot at the
  middle of its underside, so the game can push it in along the slope's
  normal (about 0.03 m) when he presses it.
- **One model, four posts:** use linked duplicates. All four face the same
  way, their buttons toward the cistern (south, +z) and their rods toward the
  door.
- **The symbols later:** each symbol will be a second layer on its button's
  face, so the post stays shared. Keep the button's face empty.
- **The guide (`exports/sewers.glb`):**
  - Each post replaces a `symbol door.button.<n> <symbol>` placeholder (an
    iron stand 0.3 m square and 1 m tall, a face on top), keeping its name
    and its `symbol` and `order` properties.
  - They stand in a row 1.3 m in front of the symbol door, 1 m apart, west to
    east: key at (2.7, -56.45), fish at (3.7, -56.45), skull at
    (4.7, -56.45), wheel at (5.7, -56.45).

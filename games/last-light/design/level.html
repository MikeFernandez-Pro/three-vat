Last Light: the level

Started 2026-10-10. Beats 1 to 3 are built in the 2D prototype, the far bank's gate and guard included. About a 5 min run. The player should finish feeling they survived. decided is what you chose. proposed is a draft to react to. open still needs a decision. reserve is kept for later.

Rules of the level

  • decided Puzzles are simple. The challenge is choosing right and managing the light.
  • decided Linear, but the player shouldn't notice: most of the time there are only one or two obvious directions.
  • decided The space feels open. Trees, rocks, streams and fallen trunks limit it, not built walls.
  • decided No fuel stock. The torch burns for a set time, and refuels at any flame. What limits the player is how far apart the flames are and how fast the torch burns.
  • decided The flame dims steadily from the moment it's lit, so how much time is left is always readable. The reach still holds full for three quarters of the burn, then shrinks over the last quarter, as today.
  • decided Timings are tuned to the last second, as in Outlast 2: one more second would have been too late.
  • decided Every mechanic is shown before it's tested. A key is seen before it's found.
  • decided The nearest flame as the crow flies is a trap: it's further on foot. Read the ground, not the glow.
  • proposed The trap works through darkness, not the camera angle: the stream between you and the near flame only shows once your light reaches it. That way it works the same in the 2D demo and in 3D.
  • decided Every obstacle comes first in a safe version: the first crawl is under a burning trunk, inside its light.
  • decided Rats are the only danger. They're never something to solve.
  • decided Events are scripted by the level. Short scripted text lines guide the player.
  • dropped Wind, gusts, shelter behind rocks, rat nests, luring.

Map: beats 1 and 2

Top-down, to scale, north up, in metres. Distances are measured against the game's values today: the torch burns 30 s, the child walks at 2 m/s, the fastest rats run at 2.3 m/s.

Beat 1: the burning tree

WhenWhat happens
0:00The child stands by the big tree, an unlit stick in hand. Dark all round. decided Control is locked for this short opening, so the strike always lands while he's beside the tree.
0:02Eyes all round in the dark, and the rats rush in.
0:05Lightning hits the big tree, 2.5 m away. It burns for good. Rats are pushed out of the clearing. Control is given.
0:06The cracked tree falls across the only gap north.
0:08A second strike sets its crown alight against the rock: a second light, and a flame to refuel at.
+6 sIf the stick is still unlit: text "The fire...". Touching the tree, within 0.8 m, lights it.
+3 sIf standing at the trunk without crawling: text "[C] Crawl".

Two ways out: north, under the trunk, toward light; east, a dark gully full of eyes that leads nowhere. Light says go, dark says no, with no sign needed.

That's for a player who walks straight. One who stops when the flame disappears behind the rock arrives with 0 to 3 s left. Here's the Outlast trick: the reach shrinks only in the last 15 m, with the flame already in view, so 4 real seconds feel like the last one. If play-testers die here, move the faint flame closer. That's a change to the level data, not to the rules.

The doubt is built in. The flame is seen at P1, then vanishes behind the tall rock at P2, just when the light has visibly dimmed. It comes back at P3, close.

What the 2D demo needs for these two beats

  • An opening with control locked, scripted.
  • A light that switches on at a set time: the two strikes. It's the fragile flame's script in reverse.
  • A wall that appears at a set time and can only be passed by crawling: the falling trunk.
  • Crawl: C as a toggle, left Ctrl held, slower: decided 1 m/s, half the walk.
  • The torch starts unlit, and lights at a flame the way it refuels.
  • A low obstacle that blocks the way but not the view: the thicket.
  • Text lines, each set off by a trigger.
  • The flame dimming steadily. In 2D, the torch's glow fades.
  • The designer overlay: seconds of light left at each route point.

proposed Camera: third person, turned by the mouse, as the game already is today, but with the pointer locked instead of click-and-drag. After a couple of seconds without mouse movement, while walking, it eases back behind the child. In a chase, the player needs to be able to look back at the swarm while running forward. The 2D demo

Built for beats 1 and 2: 2d.html, served by the game's dev server at localhost:5173/2d.html. Beat 2 there is an open wood, not the corridor drawn above. Second layout, 2026-10-10: the cracked tree 11 m from the big tree, knocked down burning by the second strike; the faint flame in a pocket between two tall rocks, so whichever way round the thicket the player goes, it is lost over the last ~16 m and seen again only rounding a rock; glowing mushrooms along both ways; rats set down at spawn spots in the bushes and the thicket that wake as the child comes near.

Beat 3, built 2026-10-10: the pocket opens north onto the river's south bank, and the bridge stands 12 m east of it, so the way on turns and the brazier is seen from the pocket's mouth. The river's edge and the bridge's rails stop the child, the rats and the light, but not the eye. The cart fills the deck. Its brazier sits toward its east side: dipped from there, never from behind. Its light covers the pusher. It jams against a tree 1.4 m past the far end, leaving room to come round it. Checkpoints: the faint flame once dipped there, then the cart's side; a catch puts the cart back where it stood. Measured for a straight walker on the keys:

decided Test the play top-down in 2D first, then move to 3D. Everything that affects gameplay stays the same: the light's reach, the burn time, the timings, the rats' speeds. Fog, bloom, models and camera moves (the beam's high view) are 3D-only and stay out.

proposed The game's rules (run.ts), the swarm (swarm.ts), the walls and the lit areas don't depend on three.js. So a Canvas page can drive them directly. The gameplay values then stay the same because it's the same code, not copied numbers. The level is written once as data, and the 3D page loads that same data later.

proposed The 2D view is dark outside the lights, and a distant flame still shows as a glow. That tests sightlines the way 3D will see them.

proposed A designer overlay shows the seconds of light left on arrival at each flame on the intended route. That's how the "last second" moments get tuned.

Open questions

  • open The safe zone: could be the glow seen from the burning tree. Decide once the level's size is known.
  • open Fog in 3D: today it hides everything far away. The beam's view of the field of flames, and any distant goal, need a new rule, for example: fog hides the ground and the rats, but flames show through it as glows.
  • open Later: whether the beam's far end is dangerous, depending on how fast the player crossed.
  • open The dark run's speed. Today the child walks at 2 m/s and the fastest rats run at 2.3 m/s, so they gain on a walking child. The run speed and the distance to the safe zone set how close they get; that's where the last second is tuned. Crawling under trunks is where they gain most.

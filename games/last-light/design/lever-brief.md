# The three levers: an art brief

A brief for ChatGPT, which draws **four separate pictures of each lever**
(front, left side, right side and back), and then for Tripo3D, which turns
each set of four pictures into a 3D model. Section 5 holds the prompts to
paste, in order, in **one chat**.

**Two models** are made for the three levers:

- the **branch lever**, made once and placed twice: at the end of the gas
  tunnel and at the end of the chandelier tunnel;
- the **hub lever**, its big brother, in front of the huge door.

This brief replaces the hub lever of the huge door's brief (its prompt 6).

The branch lever's emblem plate is drawn **blank**. Its emblems (the gas cloud
and the chandelier, as on the door's bars) are added later.

---

## 1. What they are in the game

*Last Light* is a small horror game. A kid of about 8 (1.2 m tall, with an
orange pumpkin head and a burning stick for a torch) has to escape a sewer
full of rats.

In the sewer's round central chamber, the **hub**, stands the **huge door**,
locked by three steel bars:

- **The gas lever**, at the end of the west tunnel, raises the door's left
  bar and shuts off the tunnel's gas jets. He holds it 2 s.
- **The water lever**, at the end of the east tunnel, raises the door's
  right bar and drops the tunnel's chandeliers. He holds it 2 s.
- **The hub lever**, a few metres in front of the door, lifts the central
  bar. It moves only once both side bars are up. He holds it **5 s** while the
  rats pour in: the climax of the hub.

Each lever has a lamp beside it.

What the levers need to read as:

- **the sewer's machinery:** the same blackened steel, copper pipes and brass
  as the huge door and the gas jets, so the player links each lever to the
  door;
- **worked by a pipe:** each lever's pipe goes off into the floor or along
  the wall, as if it pumped the piston of its bar;
- **heavy to pull:** a thick arm and a big grip, so holding F for seconds
  looks like hard work for a small kid;
- **the hub lever as the master:** bigger, with a toothed ratchet and a big
  gauge, so it looks like the one that comes last.

## 2. The branch lever (in metres; the kid is 1.2 m)

A **simple** prop with only four parts. Nothing else.

1. **The housing:** a squat riveted steel box with rounded edges, about
   **0.4 m wide, 0.35 m deep and 0.45 m tall**, on a bolted floor plate a
   little wider. One wide face is the **front**, the side the kid walks up to.
2. **The arm:** a thick square steel arm, **0.9 m long**, pivoting on a round
   hub on either side of the housing's top, so it swings forward and back.
   It ends in a horizontal grip, about 0.3 m wide, wrapped in dark cloth.
   **Drawn straight upright.**
3. **The emblem plate:** a round steel plate, about **0.2 m across**, bolted
   on the front of the housing, painted bone-white and **empty**: no symbol,
   no letter, no number. The emblem goes there later.
4. **The pipe:** one copper pipe, about 0.1 m across, leaving the **back** of
   the housing low down, bending once, and ending in a bolted flange about
   0.3 m behind the housing, at the floor. The tunnel's own pipe joins on
   there. It is the one detail that tells the back from the front.

Upright, the whole model is about **0.45 m wide and 1.4 m tall**. In the
game the arm rests leaning back, away from the kid, and he pulls it toward
him through upright; its grip passes his chest.

## 3. The hub lever (in metres)

The same family, a size bigger. Only five parts. Nothing else.

1. **The housing:** a heavy riveted steel pump housing with rounded edges,
   about **0.6 m wide, 0.5 m deep and 0.6 m tall**, on a bolted floor plate.
   The **front** faces the kid, who stands on the fire's side.
2. **The arm:** a thicker square steel arm, **1.1 m long**, on a big round
   hub on either side of the housing's top, swinging forward and back. It
   ends in a horizontal grip about **0.5 m wide**, for two small hands,
   wrapped in dark cloth. **Drawn straight upright.**
3. **The ratchet:** a toothed steel arc on the housing's **right side only**,
   following the arm's swing, with a heavy pawl that clicks along its teeth.
   It tells the right side from the left.
4. **The gauge:** one big round pressure gauge, about **0.25 m across**, on
   the front of the housing, in dull dark brass with a pale face and one
   needle. **No numbers or text on its face.**
5. **The pipe:** one thick copper pipe, about 0.15 m across, leaving the
   **back** of the housing low down and bending down into the floor through a
   bolted flange. Under the floor it would run to the door, where the central
   bar's pipe comes up.

Upright, the whole model is about **0.7 m wide and 1.75 m tall**. In the game
the arm rests leaning back, toward the door, and he hauls it toward him; its
grip passes over his head, so he hangs his weight on it.

## 4. Style and colour

- **Style:** a stylized, hand-painted game prop, chunky and slightly
  exaggerated, like Dofus or *Penguins of Madagascar* concept art. Thick arms,
  big rivets, big simple shapes, few details, clean silhouette. The same
  family as the huge door and the gas jets.
- **Colours:**
  - **steel:** dark blackened steel, wet and grimy;
  - **pipes:** old copper with heavy teal-green verdigris, dark steel
    flanges;
  - **grip:** dark cloth, worn;
  - **gauge:** dull dark brass with a pale face;
  - **emblem plate:** bone-white paint, the brightest value on the branch
    lever;
  - limescale and dark water stains at the foot, a little moss.
- **Leave out:** orange, rust, gold, handwheels, text, numbers, symbols, and
  any ornaments.

## 5. The prompts

Paste them one after the other in **the same chat**, so ChatGPT keeps each
lever the same from picture to picture, and the two levers alike.

### Prompt 1: the branch lever's front view, and how the pictures work

> I need reference pictures of 3D game props so that a 3D tool (Tripo3D) can
> rebuild them. For each prop I will ask you for **four separate pictures,
> one at a time, each in its own image**: the FRONT, the LEFT side, the RIGHT
> side and the BACK. Please do NOT put several views in one image, and do not
> make a sheet or a grid.
>
> The rules for every picture:
> - exactly the same object, same size and same position in the frame, centred
>   and fully visible from the floor to the top, nothing cropped;
> - a perfectly straight-on orthographic view, with no perspective, no
>   three-quarter angle, and the camera at the object's mid-height;
> - a plain light-grey background, flat even lighting, no cast shadows, no
>   environment, no other objects, no text or labels.
>
> The first prop: a lever from an old underground sewer's machinery, in a
> stylized hand-painted game-asset style (like Dofus), chunky, with big simple
> shapes and few details. It has only four parts:
> 1. a squat riveted steel box with rounded edges, about 0.4 m wide, 0.35 m
>    deep and 0.45 m tall, on a bolted floor plate a little wider;
> 2. a thick square steel lever arm, 0.9 m long, pivoting on a round hub on
>    either side of the box's top, standing STRAIGHT UPRIGHT, ending in a
>    horizontal grip wrapped in dark cloth;
> 3. a round steel plate bolted on the FRONT of the box, painted bone-white
>    and completely EMPTY: no symbol, no letter, no number, no drawing;
> 4. one copper pipe leaving the BACK of the box low down, bending once, and
>    ending in a bolted flange on the floor a little behind the box.
>
> Colours: dark blackened wet steel, copper with heavy teal-green verdigris,
> dark worn cloth on the grip, limescale and dark water stains at the foot, a
> little moss. No orange, no rust, no gold, no handwheels, no ornaments.
>
> Now draw **picture 1 of 4: the FRONT view**, looking straight at the face
> with the white plate, the arm rising up the middle of the picture.

that picture again. Tripo3D needs each lever's four views to match.

## 6. After Tripo3D (Blender)

- **The pivot** of each model goes at the middle of its floor plate, on the
  floor.
- **The arm** is cut apart from the housing in Blender, its pivot on the axis
  through the two side hubs. The game swings it:
  - the branch lever: from about 40° back (away from the kid) to about 40°
    forward;
  - the hub lever: from about 40° back (toward the door) to about 50°
    forward, slowly, over the 5 s hold.
- **The hub lever's pawl** is cut apart too, so it can tick along the teeth.
- **One branch lever, two places:** use a linked duplicate. Each will get its
  own emblem later, as a second layer on the white plate.
- **The guide (`exports/sewers.glb`):** each lever replaces its placeholder
  (an iron post 0.2 m square and 1 m tall with a handle on top), keeping its
  name and its `hold` property.
  - `lever.gas` at (-33.25, -11.2), the end of the gas tunnel, its front
    facing east, toward the hub; its back and pipe's flange toward the
    tunnel's end wall, where the main gas pipe along the walls joins on.
  - `lever.water` at (39.27, -11.2), the end of the chandelier tunnel, its
    front facing west, toward the hub.
  - `lever.hub` at (0, -15.6), its front facing south, toward the fire, its
    back and pipe toward the huge door.
  - Each lever's lamp stays where the guide has it.

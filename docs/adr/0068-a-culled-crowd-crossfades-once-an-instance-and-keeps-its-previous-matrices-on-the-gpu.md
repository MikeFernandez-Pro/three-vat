# A culled crowd crossfades once an instance, and keeps its previous matrices on the GPU

> **Amends [ADR-0067](./0067-a-crowd-is-culled-on-the-gpu-where-the-renderer-can.md)**, whose GPU route walked a crossfade once a vertex and drew motion vectors that placed the previous pose at this frame's matrix, and under which a write was flagged for upload as it was made.

On the GPU route a culled crowd now holds a crossfade pass of its own, as a carrier holds one (ADR-0059, ADR-0062): the pass writes each instance's blended slots at its row, and the vertex stage reads them at the logical index it took out of the survivor list. Its velocity build places the previous frame's pose by the previous frame's matrices, read at the same logical index, out of a second storage buffer the crowd copies its matrices into on the GPU. Nothing changes on the batch route, whose `BatchedMesh`es already had both. Spec #199, ticket #203.

## Why

ADR-0067 decoded with a `logicalIndex` and no carrier, so the decode had no owner to hold a crossfade pass and walked the chain once a vertex, the cost ADR-0059 measured at about nine times a steady crowd's frame with every instance mid-fade, and the millisecond the gate measured on the iPhone (#200). And a velocity build had no previous matrices: its previous pose was placed at this frame's matrix, so a moved instance drew no motion.

Measured on the bench outside the repo, three r187, three-vat 4cae9bc with this change, RTX 5080, WebGPU, 1600x900, dpr 1, every Soldier mid-crossfade for the whole run, best GPU frame, median of three interleaved rounds: 1,024 drew in 1.22 ms against 1.26 for a `BatchedMesh` crowd, and 4,096 in 4.64 against 4.74. With the walk forced back into the vertex shader, as ADR-0067 drew it, the same crowd took 11.33 and 45.21 ms.

Motion vectors, by the velocity bench of ADR-0064, three r187, WebGPU, one character over two frames 0.05 s apart: the culled crowd lands where a `createVATMesh` crowd does, to the decimal, off three's mixer by 4.8% (Soldier walk), 5.2% (run), 4.7% (crossfade), 1.5%, 9.1% and 3.3% (Imp); a move adds 0.8% (Soldier) and 5.9% (Imp) more than a plain mesh moved as far. With its previous matrices dropped the same move is off by 599% and 613%.

## How it runs

- **The pass's owner.** `vatNodes` keys a crowd's crossfade passes on its carrier and frees them when the carrier sends `dispose`. The culled crowd hands the decode an owner of its own in its place, through an internal export, `vatNodesFadingOn`; the crowd's `dispose()` sends it. One owner for every mesh and pass of the crowd, so one pass serves them all, once a moment, as on a carrier. The pass writes at an instance's row, which is its logical index, so reading there needs nothing new. A caller's own `logicalIndex` (ADR-0051) names no owner and still walks once a vertex.
- **The previous matrices** are made by the first build that draws velocity and placed by the logical index, as this frame's are. They are copied from the matrices at the next cull, whatever moved: the build may come before the crowd is placed, in a compile ahead of the first frame, and the array may hold writes not yet uploaded, so they start as the GPU holds the matrices; and the copy compiles then, not at the first move.
- **Copied on the GPU, at the cull.** A compute pass three dispatches during a render is submitted ahead of that render's draws, so a copy made after a draw would run before it. The copy runs instead at the next `updateVATCulledCrowd`, ahead of the cull, while the GPU still holds the matrices the last frame drew: **a write is now flagged for upload at the next cull, not as it is made**, so the copy reads the buffer before the frame's writes reach it. After that it runs only after a cull that uploaded a move: with nothing moved since, the two buffers already agree.
- **The route counts the buffers.** The GPU route reads up to five storage buffers in the vertex stage for a rig bake (the matrices, the survivor list, the crossfade poses, and in a velocity build the previous matrices and poses), three for the vertex encoding, which has no crossfade pass. A device reporting fewer takes the batch route. WebGPU's core limit is eight.

## Considered and not taken

- **Copying the previous matrices on the CPU**, as ADR-0064's storage route does for an `InstancedMesh`. It uploads the whole buffer every frame something moves, the upload the culled crowd exists to avoid.
- **Swapping two buffers each frame** in place of the copy. The CPU writes into one array; both buffers would then need every write, uploaded twice.
- **Flagging a write as it is made, and copying ahead of it some other way.** three uploads a storage buffer whenever a pass that binds it is dispatched, the copy included; holding the flag until the cull is the one place that orders the copy before the upload.
- **A crossfade pass for every caller's `logicalIndex`.** Such a caller has no object of the library's to own the pass and free it; ADR-0051 leaves the pipeline to it.

## Consequences

- **One cull a frame.** A second `updateVATCulledCrowd` in a frame, for a second camera, copies the first's upload, and the second view draws a moved instance with no motion. ADR-0067 already asks for one call a frame.
- **A write shows at the next cull.** A write made after `updateVATCulledCrowd` and before the render is drawn a frame late; `updateVATCulledCrowd` was already to be called after the frame's writes. The span uploaded is the one written since the last cull.
- **Memory:** two texels of storage a slot of every row for the pass (ADR-0059), and where velocity is drawn a second pass's and a second copy of the matrices, 64 bytes an instance.
- **Coverage.** CI drives the public functions on the stand-in renderer: the vertex stage walks no chain and reads the pass's poses at the instance it takes out of the survivor list; a velocity build reads the previous matrices there too, beside the previous pass's poses, and a build without velocity reads neither; the copy runs at the first cull after the first velocity build and at the cull after a move, before the move is flagged for upload, and not after a still frame; a vertex-encoding crowd builds no pass; the route counts five buffers for a rig and three for the vertex encoding; `dispose` frees the passes' storage, the previous matrices and the copy. The bench (outside the repo, three r187, RTX 5080, Chrome, dpr 1, 1600x900) drew 1,024 turned and scaled Soldiers, every one 0.2 s into a 0.5 s crossfade, through the culled crowd and through `createVATMesh` at a fixed clock: all in view, half out, with shadows, and over two materials. The GPU route drew the same pixels in every case, not one differing; the batch route, on the WebGL 2 fallback and on WebGPU told its device has no vertex-stage storage, differed on at most 3 pixels, none past the check's threshold. With its crossfade pass never run, the culled crowd differed on 8-16% of the frame. The velocity bench is above.
- **Unmeasured:** the iPhone, where ADR-0067's gate put about a millisecond on the crossfade walk; and the unexplained millisecond in its instanced draw.

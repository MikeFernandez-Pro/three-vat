# A culled crowd chooses its levels by the rule of `updateVATLevels`

> Completes [ADR-0067](./0067-a-crowd-is-culled-on-the-gpu-where-the-renderer-can.md), which left the culled crowd one level and refused an atlas.

`createVATCulledCrowd(renderer, lods.vat, instances, { levels: lods.levels, bands, slack })` takes the levels `createVATLODs` returned and chooses each instance's level at `updateVATCulledCrowd`, by the bands and the remembered-level slack of `updateVATLevels` (ADR-0065), on both routes. An atlas is no longer refused: the option `characters` says which character each instance is, and an instance keeps it at every level. Spec #199, ticket #202.

## One rule, two routes

- **The batch route runs `updateVATLevels` itself.** One `BatchedMesh` holds every level, level by level and character by character within a level, in `createVATLODs`'s order; each instance starts at the full detail of its character, and `updateVATCulledCrowd` moves it.
- **The GPU route runs the same rule in the cull.** Each instance keeps its level in a buffer of its own, one `uint` an instance, every one at the full detail before the first cull. The cull moves it out past each band it is `slack × band` beyond, then back in past each it is `slack × band` within, so a camera cut lands every instance where it belongs in one cull, as `updateVATLevels` does. The thresholds are literals in the kernel: the bands and the slack are fixed when the crowd is made. The distance is from the camera's world position to the instance's, its matrix's translation through the crowd's world matrix, as `updateVATLevels` measures it.
- **Every instance's level is chosen, hidden and out of view too,** because `updateVATLevels` moves every live instance of a batch, shown or not. An instance that walks back into view is then on the level the batch route would have it on, its slack included.
- **One list a level and character** (ADR-0057): the survivors are appended to list `level × characters + character`, each list the capacity long, its count in its own record of indirect arguments. **Each list is one mesh**, drawing it indirectly, its vertex stage reading the instance out of its own list at its drawn slot, from `j × capacity` on: a non-zero `firstInstance` in an indirect draw needs an optional WebGPU feature (ADR-0057). A bake without levels keeps one list and one mesh a material group, as before. The crossfade pass is one for the crowd, however many lists read it (ADR-0068).
- **A list's geometry is the VAT's own vertices**, under the level's index. An atlas character's index is moved to where the character starts in the atlas's geometry, so under the vertex encoding each vertex reads its own column at its vertex index; `vat.lods`'s modulo (ADR-0043) leaves an index below the width unchanged.

## An atlas

`characters` gives one character an instance, up to the capacity; an instance not given is character 0. It is fixed for the crowd's life, as the capacity is (ADR-0022), so four functions stay four: an instance hidden and shown again for a respawn keeps its character. A setter for it would be a fifth function and a level read back on the batch route; no caller has asked. The GPU route keeps the characters in a buffer the cull reads, the batch route adds each instance at its character's full detail, and from there `updateVATLevels` keeps it.

An atlas's VAT holds every character's materials end to end, and its geometry a group each. A batch takes one material, so an atlas `BatchedMesh` draws every character in one (usage: "Several characters in one batch"); the culled crowd draws an atlas with its first material on both routes, so the two routes draw the same.

## Refused

- **Levels of a bake of more than one material.** A level from `createVATLODs` has one index and no groups, so nothing says which of its triangles is which material. The spec's "a multi-material VAT draws once per material per level" cannot be kept; a bake of several materials rides the culled crowd without levels.
- **Levels that are not the VAT's**: levels with a VAT `createVATLODs` did not return with them, a VAT with levels given none, a level of the wrong number of characters. **Levels without bands.** **Bands and slack** that `updateVATLevels` refuses, checked once at creation by the same function. **A character** the VAT does not have, and more characters than the capacity.

## Considered and not taken

- **Levels chosen only for the instances in view.** Cheaper by a few instructions an instance, but a culled instance would keep a stale level and could come back into view inside the slack on a level the batch route would not draw.
- **Bands that change after creation.** As uniforms they could; nothing asks yet, and a literal is what the tests read.
- **The level read back, or `onChange`.** The GPU route cannot call back without a readback, which the spec leaves out of scope.
- **Each atlas character in its own colour from its own material.** It would split one batch into one a character on the batch route, and an atlas batch is one draw by design (ADR-0040).

## Consequences

- **An atlas is culled by the atlas's bounds** on the GPU route, every character's, where the batch route culls each character by its own: a small character near the view's edge is kept a little longer, drawing nothing different.
- **Memory on the GPU route:** the survivor lists grow to the capacity times the levels times the characters, four bytes an entry; the levels and the characters four bytes an instance each.
- **The kernel binds up to six storage buffers** (the matrices, the visibility, the lists, the arguments, the levels, the characters), under WebGPU's eight; the vertex stage reads no more than before.
- **Coverage.** CI drives the public functions on the stand-in renderer: the batch route holding every level and moving each instance by band and slack either way, keeping its level between updates; an atlas instance on its given character at every level, on both routes; one mesh a level over its index, its arguments carrying its index count, its vertex stage reading its own list; the cull reading each instance's kept level, crossing the bands at `band × (1 ± slack)` and writing it back, and choosing nothing without bands; each refusal above. The bench (outside the repo, three 0.187, RTX 5080, Chrome, dpr 1, 1600x900) drew 1,024 turned and scaled Soldiers, rig encoding, at three levels — the whole Soldier, every second triangle, every fourth — with bands splitting the crowd 408, 334 and 282, against a `BatchedMesh` of the same levels chosen by `updateVATLevels` and culling no instance in any pass, at a fixed clock and camera: all in view, half out, with shadows, and with a shadow camera seeing only a corner of the crowd. The GPU route, the batch route on the WebGL 2 fallback, and the batch route on WebGPU told its device has no vertex-stage storage drew no pixel differing by more than the check's threshold of 24 levels summed over red, green and blue, in any case, and at most 2 pixels differing at all; the levels changed 3.3-3.7% of the frame against the full detail, and bands 5% wider in the culled crowd than in the reference made 0.5-0.9% of it differ on both routes. An atlas of Soldier and the UAL1 mannequin, 15,980 vertices, its two characters alternating instance by instance, each at the same three levels, drew the reference's pixels the same way under the rig encoding and under the vertex encoding, where each vertex's column hangs on the index moved to its character's first vertex: none past the threshold, at most 2 differing at all, the levels changing 2.9-3.3% of the frame; the culled crowd told every instance is the first character differed on 2.6-6.3% of it. The GPU route kept exactly the instances in view. The WebGPU batch of several geometries with the narrow shadow camera drew the reference's pixels, so ADR-0067's worry from r186 does not reproduce on 0.187. ADR-0067's and ADR-0068's checks, run again on this code, passed.
- **Unmeasured:** the levels' cost against a `BatchedMesh` crowd with levels, and the phone.
